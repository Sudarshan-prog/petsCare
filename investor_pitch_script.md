## ACT 5: OUR SOLUTION — CAREBRIDGE (90 seconds)

>
> Here's how it works. I'll walk you through a real user flow.

## ACT 9: HANDLING INVESTOR Q&A

### If they ask: *"How does your tech actually work under the hood?"*

> "Our architecture is a **hybrid serverless model**:
>
> - **Frontend:** Flutter (Dart) — single codebase for Android + iOS
> - **Backend:** Firebase Cloud Functions (Node.js) — 5 functions handling all sensitive operations
> - **Database:** Cloud Firestore with real-time listeners — changes propagate to every connected device instantly
> - **Payments:** Razorpay SDK with server-side webhook verification
> - **Notifications:** Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) with foreground + background handlers
> - **Storage:** Firebase Storage for live booking photos
> - **Security:** Firestore Security Rules enforce field-level access control — a user cannot modify another user's rating or booking status directly
>
> The key architectural decision: **all money-related operations happen server-side**. The phone never calculates the final price. The Cloud Function does. This prevents any form of payment tampering."

---

### If they ask: *"What about your Cloud Functions? What exactly do they do?"*

> "We have 5 deployed Cloud Functions:
>
> 1. **createBooking** — Validates the booking, calculates fees server-side (2.5% + 2.5%), sends push notification to caretaker
> 2. **updateBookingStatus** — Enforces a strict state machine: pending → confirmed → completed. No skipping. If a payment is involved, it handles Razorpay capture/refund
> 3. **submitRating** — Calculates the caretaker's new weighted average rating, prevents duplicate ratings, sends notification to caretaker
> 4. **deleteAccount** — GDPR-compliant. Cancels all active bookings, deletes pets, purges storage photos, removes Firestore documents, and finally deletes the Firebase Auth account
> 5. **razorpayWebhook** — Receives Razorpay POST events (payment.authorized, payment.captured, payment.failed, refund.processed), verifies HMAC signature, and updates booking payment status accordingly"

---

### If they ask: *"What if a caretaker is abusive or negligent?"*

> "Three layers of protection:
>
> 1. **Live photo updates** — The caretaker is required to upload photos during the session. The owner sees them in real-time. This creates accountability
> 2. **Server-side ratings** — After every completed booking, the owner rates the caretaker. The rating is calculated by our Cloud Function as a weighted average — it cannot be manipulated client-side. A pattern of low ratings makes the caretaker unsearchable
> 3. **Planned: KYC verification** — In our 90-day roadmap, we're implementing Aadhaar-based caretaker verification. Unverified caretakers will be clearly marked, giving owners the choice"

---

### If they ask: *"Why not just build on an existing platform like Uber's model?"*

> "Pet care is fundamentally different from ride-hailing in one critical way: **trust takes longer to build**. You don't care about your Uber driver's personality — you care about getting from A to B. But when someone is in your home with your pet for 4 hours, you need to **trust them deeply**.
>
> That's why we built live photo updates, server-verified ratings, and a transparent fee model. These aren't features Uber or Ola's infrastructure supports. This needed to be purpose-built."

### If they ask: *"How do you plan to prevent caretakers from taking bookings outside the app?"*

> "This is the classic marketplace disintermediation risk. Our defense is a **value stack** that's hard to replicate outside the app:
>
> 1. **Payment protection** — The owner's money is only released to the caretaker after the job is completed
> 2. **Rating history** — A caretaker's rating is their resume. Off-platform bookings don't build their profile
> 3. **Push notifications** — Instant alerts for new bookings, confirmations, and ratings keep caretakers engaged
> 4. **Planned: Loyalty tiers** — Higher-rated caretakers get priority placement in search results. That visibility only exists on CareBridge"
